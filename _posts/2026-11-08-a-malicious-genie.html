--- title: A Malicious Genie date: 2026-11-08 23:17:56 +0000 description: "Discussion Question: What would you ask from a malicious genie?" source_url: https://bradygho.substack.com/p/a-malicious-genie ---

Question: What would you ask from a malicious genie?

Brody: My interpretation of this question is that a genie’s maliciousness is proportional to how complex or greedy your wish is. For example, if you wished for an axe, the genie probably wouldn’t try to kill you by spawning the axe on your head. But if you asked the genie to make someone else love you, things would probably go wrong.

Chris: My first thoughts was a proof for P=NP.

Aayan: Basically we need to ask it for something simple but powerful. The most obvious answer is money?

Brody: I agree we should ask for something simple, but I don’t actually think money is that powerful? Even with 1 billion dollars, there are many things you can’t do.

Aayan: Wait, money is pretty powerful though? For example, SpaceX exists only because Elon was able to sustain it with a ton of money during its early years.

Ian: You could also always ask for more money if a billion isn’t enough. Just ask for 100 billion, or even a trillion.

Brody: We’re still thinking too limited. The way I think about it is, there are problems in the realm of possibility and problems in the realm of impossibility. It would be a waste not to focus on the later category of problems

Aayan: The issue is that those types of problems are also normally complex, and have high potential for downside?

Chris: That’s why we need to choose carefully.

Brody: I think P=NP is a problem is a good candidate problem to ask the genie about. It’s “intangible” in the sense that even if you had 1 billion dollars, you likely wouldn’t be able to solve. I also can’t think of any crazy downsides.

Chris: Yeah, the only downside I can think of is that the proof the genie gives us is long and incomprehensible.

Ian: The issue is that even if we have the proof for P=NP, it’s not actually super useful. In fact, we’ll likely just get a proof that P≠NP, which most people already assume to be true.

Brody: Agree. What if we direct our thinking towards physics problems?

Chris: Like “build me a working quantum computer?”

Aayan: Ohh that’s good. We could also do something energy related? In many ways we’re always energy-bottlenecked.

Ian: What about a working fusion reactor?

Brody: I was also thinking we could also ask clarifying questions like “how many dimensions does this universe contain?” or “what is dark matter?”

Chris: or “How did the universe come to be?”

Brody: I guess the most obvious downside of that is that the genie will probably make you go insane.

Chris: We could also ask the genie to “stop the expansion of the universe” so we could make it out of our local supercluster.

Aayan: Or faster than light travel

Brody: Wait this is lowkey a bad idea. We really don’t understand physics well enough to confidently say this won’t cause irrepairable damage to the universe. I say we shouldn’t try to mess with the laws of physics.

Aayan: Agree, and also the impacts of this wish would be 100 million years in the future. I would rather have a wish which I could see in my lifetime.

Ian: We could ask “is there aliens?” Or better yet, “where are aliens?”

Chris: that’s really good!

Brody: What about life extension? What if we ask for everyone’s lifespan doubled? Or, we could ask to extend our years of youth or something.

Chris: Well, the genie could turns us all into turtles or something.

Brody: Ohh, you’re right. Yeah, messing with biology in general is extremely dangerous.

Chris: I just thought of something—there’s also this number called Chaitin’s number. There’s an equivalence where knowing the first N bits of Chaitin’s number means you solve the halting problem for all programs up to length N.

Chris: We could then ask the genie for the first trillion digits of Chaitin’s number. A good application of this would be to solve NP-hard problems for any reasonably sized programs/inputs, since all NP-hard problems reduce to halt.

Brody: I get the sense that in general, good things to ask the genie are existence/knowledge-based questions. These questions have the highest likelihood of not having downsides, while creating an intangible amount of value.

Brody: Does anyone want to lock in any final answers before we end?

Chris: Either “the first 10 petabyte of Chiton’s number” or “how did the universe begin?”

Ian: I’ll lock in “where are the Aliens?”

Aayan: I’m not totally sure; maybe something energy related.

Brody: “how did the universe begin” seems a bit too dangerous. Probably something more simple, like “how old is the universe,” “how many dimensions does this universe have,” or “what is dark matter.”
