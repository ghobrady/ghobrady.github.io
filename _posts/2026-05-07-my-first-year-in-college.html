My first year at CMU was good, but not great. There’s plenty to be grateful for and many other things that have left me wanting more.

The Positive

I’m most certainly going to write another post reviewing the classes I’ve taken these last two semesters, so I’ll spare the specifics; however, I’ll briefly highlight some of my favorites at CMU. 21-242: Honors Linear Algebra is now my all-time favorite class. The content deeply fundamental to many areas of math, and I loved Professor Wesley Pegden’s lecturing style (from North Carolina Gerrymandering trials). I’d also like to mention 15-251: Theoretical Computer Science, 21-269: Multivariate Calculus/Real Analysis, and 15-150: Functional Programming for drastically shaping the ways I think about math/CS.

Overall, CMU academics blew all expectations out of the water. Course topics, lectures, homework, course support, and class community—these were all magnitudes better than any class I’ve taken at high school or community college.

I was also pleasantly surprised by level of technical competence and passion for math/CS at CMU. The water level was high enough that for the first time in my life, I was swimming instead of standing on the shoreline—and this made me really excited!

This higher water level helped me realize many of my technical weaknesses and motivated me to grow. For example, I really admire my friend Rushil Surti for his persistent curiosity in Math/CS topics. His passion has spilled over to me—after two semesters listening to his lunch rambles, I’m a lot more excited to learn about topics such as abstract algebra, category theory/functional programming, and TCS. I’d also like to call out my roommate, Siddharth Radhakrishnan, for teaching me a lot about computer systems, hardware, and Linux, as well as building my confidence and comfortability around large systems I don’t understand.

The things I wished for

25-26 might be in contention for the year of least emotional/philosophical growth. There’s many explanations for why this might be the case:

  • Academics was very time consuming and energy draining (especially second semester).

  • Most catalysts for my past emotional growth came from social interaction, which happened much less than previous years i.e. I mainly stuck with talking with the same ~40 people the whole school year and didn’t have particular romantic interests

  • I know very few people (who I’m comfortable around) that seriously thinks/discusses philosophy, ethics or social dynamics

  • I’ve matured past the point where I should expect significant emotional/philosophical growth every year

I did have a few milestones/moments of growth—i.e. I made my first EA pledge donation with the paycheck I received last summer (this was a surprisingly hard emotional hurdle to overcome), and I’ve been in the talks with my friends, Stephen Zhang and Vinay Singamsetty, about philosophy/EA/ethics. However, my year overall had no big emotional lessons.

School has also had some disappointments, particularly second semester. I was really hoping to become a TA for the class 15-251: Theoretical Computer Science both because I enjoyed the content and because I was friends with many existing TAs. Failing final round interviews made me reflect deeply about some of my personal faults; for instance, I want to fix my stutter-y, filler-y style of speech, and I hope to improve my ability to flatten the “knowledge manifold” in my head into a linear, speakable explanation.1

Grades also did not “work out” like in high school. Many weeks I felt overwhelmed by the nonstop waves of schoolwork, and this was worsened by my bad coping mechanisms of stress-eating and procrastination. I did badly enough on midterms/finals for some classes that I ended up getting 2 B’s this semester. There’s definitely a lesson here that I haven’t internalized—something something fix bad habits and don’t put too much on the plate?

In Summary

There’s a lot to aim for during the summer and future semesters. In the short term, I hope to continue my explorations of computers/linux, and maybe learn more math as well (category theory/abstract algebra?). For communication, someone suggested I record myself speaking and listen back to it. I’m also going to read and write more, both for communication improvement and emotional/philosophical growth.

While I did really enjoy all the technical material I learned this year, I want have better balance in future semesters. I also hope to work on some of my bad coping mechanisms—which I will admit is a tall tasks since I’ve been trying to fix them this since the start of high school, with little progress.

This is my first blog post—so please excuse any rough writing. This was more general-life-update style, and I’m hoping to write more subject/idea specific pieces in the future. If you have any feedback, please message me on my socials!
